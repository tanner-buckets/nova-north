Chaos Cup

A Pokémon Trading Card Game tournament with the rules bent. All you need is a Standard legal deck — what changes is what the game does with it.

The rules below are examples, not the rules

Every Chaos Cup has its own set, written for that day. The rules on this page are ones we have played with before, here to show you the kind of thing to expect. Do not build a deck around any of them. What is in play is announced at the event, round by round.

How a Chaos Cup runs

  • You need a Standard legal deck. That is the whole requirement. It is a normal tournament underneath — Swiss rounds, the same entry, the same deck legality as any other event.
  • Each round has its own rules. They are announced at the start of the round and they apply to both players.
  • Sometimes they build on each other. One Chaos Cup might wipe the slate between rounds and start clean. Another might follow a single theme — everybody drawing far too many cards, say, or Trainer cards behaving strangely — and turn it up each round, stacking a new rule on top of the last.
  • How the rules get picked varies. Some are drawn at random on the spot, some are put to a vote, and some are chosen by the professors beforehand. Which of those is happening is part of the format for the day.
  • Some are themed. A Chaos Cup near a holiday, or alongside something else going on at the league, may have a set written to match it.
  • Sometimes the rules reward a strange deck. Most of the time your usual deck is the right answer. Occasionally a set is written so that building something unusual pays off — and when that is the plan, it is worth asking a professor before the day rather than finding out in round one.

A judge is on hand all day, and a Chaos Cup is exactly the kind of afternoon where you should ask rather than guess. If a rule and a card seem to contradict each other, the rule for the round wins unless the judge says otherwise.

Some rules we have played with

Six out of dozens, picked to show the range. Some change how you draw, some change how you win, and at least one involves a hand of Rock Paper Scissors.

  • Double or nothing

    Once during your turn you may discard your hand and roll a six-sided die. Draw twice as many cards as you rolled.

  • Swapped Trainers

    Treat Supporters as Items and Items as Supporters. You may play only one Item per turn, and as many Supporters as you like. Going first, you still may not play an Item on your first turn.

  • Twelve Prizes

    Set up twelve Prize cards instead of six, and always take double Prizes after a knockout.

  • Abilities cost you the spot

    Whenever a player uses an Ability during their turn, that player must switch their Active Pokémon to the Bench.

  • Settle it by hand

    After you attack, play Rock Paper Scissors with your opponent. Whoever loses, their Active Pokémon is now Burned.

  • Backwards Prizes

    If you would take a Prize card, your opponent takes one instead. The first player to take all of their Prizes loses.

Others have put every evolved Pokémon back to the HP of its Basic, made you play the whole round with your hand face up, or had you trade your deck for your discard pile halfway through. The list grows every time somebody suggests something.

Coming to one

Chaos Cups appear on the schedule like any other event, and a Standard legal deck is all you need to bring. If one takes pre-registration you can sign up from its own page.
